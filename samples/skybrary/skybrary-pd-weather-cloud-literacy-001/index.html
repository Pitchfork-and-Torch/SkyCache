Cloud and Weather Literacy (Educational Primer)

Cumulus clouds are often puffy fair-weather heaps. Towering cumulonimbus can mean heavy rain, wind, and lightning. Stratus layers may bring long drizzle. Cirrus are high thin ice-crystal clouds that can precede weather changes.

Community practices:
1. Watch how wind and cloud type change through the day.
2. Record simple rain and drought notes for farming memory.
3. Take shelter from lightning and flash floods per local guidance.

Open free-to-air weather imagery (where lawful) can complement ground observation. This primer is educational literacy, not a forecast product.
