Germ Theory for the Public (Historical Science Literacy)

IMPORTANT: Educational history of science only. NOT diagnostic lab guidance.

In the nineteenth century, Pasteur and contemporaries showed that many fermentations and diseases relate to microscopic life. Public teaching that followed stressed:

1. Invisible organisms can spoil food and spread illness.
2. Heat can kill many microbes (pasteurization of milk became a classic example).
3. Cleanliness of instruments and dressings reduces infection risk in care settings.
4. Separating sick animals or people when advised can slow outbreaks (quarantine literacy).

Modern medicine uses vaccines, antibiotics, sterile technique, and laboratory diagnosis taught by professionals. This text is heritage science literacy for Skybrary kits.
