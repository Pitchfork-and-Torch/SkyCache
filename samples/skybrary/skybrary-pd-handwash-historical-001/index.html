Hand Hygiene Principles (Historical Educational Summary)

IMPORTANT: Educational literacy only. NOT medical advice. Follow current local health authority guidance for clinical practice.

Semmelweis and later nursing literature stressed that clean hands reduce the spread of disease between patients and within households. Historical manuals taught:

1. Wash hands before preparing food and after using the latrine.
2. Wash hands before and after caring for a sick person when water and soap are available.
3. Use clean water when possible; if water is scarce, prioritize handwashing for caregivers.
4. Dry hands on a clean cloth when available.
5. Teach children the same habits by example.

Modern clinical settings use updated protocols, alcohol-based rubs when appropriate, and sterile technique taught by professionals. This summary is community literacy, not hospital procedure.
