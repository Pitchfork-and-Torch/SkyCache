Simple Wound Care Literacy (Historical Educational Summary)

IMPORTANT: Educational only. Deep, dirty, animal, or heavily bleeding wounds need trained care. Not tetanus protocol. Not surgical instruction.

Public-domain first-aid literature commonly taught community helpers to:

1. Stop severe bleeding with firm direct pressure using the cleanest cloth available.
2. Rinse dirt from minor cuts with clean water when available; avoid harsh folk chemicals.
3. Cover with a clean dressing; change if soaked or dirty.
4. Watch for spreading redness, fever, or pus - seek skilled care.
5. Do not close deep wounds with improvised stitching.

Vaccination and antibiotic decisions belong to licensed clinicians.
